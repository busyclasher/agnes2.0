# SafePoint Design System

## Product Feel

SafePoint should feel:

- immediate
- clear
- protective
- practical
- calm
- worker-friendly

It should not feel:

- corporate-heavy
- surveillance-like
- legalistic
- cluttered
- overly technical

## Design Principles

1. **Worker first**
   - The interface serves the worker at the hazard point.

2. **Action over explanation**
   - Every result should say what to do next.

3. **Icons plus words**
   - Use pictograms for low-literacy support, but always include text labels.

4. **Audio supported**
   - Critical guidance should be spoken.

5. **Safety without panic**
   - Red means danger, but wording should stay calm and direct.

## Main Components

- Camera scan button
- Language selector
- Risk badge
- Safety result card
- PPE icon list
- Action step list
- Pictogram card
- Audio playback button
- Confidence note
- Report incident button

## Risk Badge Rules

| Risk | Label | UX Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Information | Safe/informational guidance |
| Yellow | Caution | Check before continuing |
| Red | Danger | Stop, check, or seek supervisor guidance |
| Unknown | Unclear | Retake photo or ask supervisor |

Do not rely on colour alone. Always include label and icon.

## Copywriting Rules

Use simple, direct language.

Good:

> “Do not enter unless authorised.”

Good:

> “Wear safety harness.”

Avoid:

> “Entry into this demarcated zone requires site-specific authorisation.”

## Screen Layout

### Scan Result Card

Recommended order:

1. Risk level
2. Plain instruction
3. PPE needed
4. Translated explanation
5. Audio button
6. Pictogram card
7. Retake / Save / Report

## Accessibility

- Large buttons
- High contrast
- Text labels
- Audio output
- Minimal scrolling
- No small icon-only controls
- No hover-only states
- Support screen readers where possible

## Pictogram Style

Pictograms should be:

- simple
- high contrast
- easy to understand
- not overly detailed
- culturally neutral
- clearly related to the hazard

Avoid decorative illustrations that make the warning unclear.

## Tone

SafePoint should sound like a good site buddy:

- clear
- firm
- respectful
- not patronising
- not alarmist

Example:

> “Danger. This area has a fall hazard. Do not enter unless authorised. Wear your safety harness.”
